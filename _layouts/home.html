Yifan Yang

Assistant Professor of Linguistics
Shanghai Jiao Tong University

I am a phonologist working at the interface of phonology, phonetics, and morphology. My research focuses on nonconcatenative morphology, reduplication, syllable structure, and the sound patterns of understudied Chinese dialects. I combine formal analysis with linguistic fieldwork and acoustic and articulatory methods. In Mandarin Chinese, my name is pronounced [i˥.fan˧˥.jɑŋ˧˥].

About

More about me

My work often combines phonological theories with empirical data collected through fieldwork and phonetic analysis, aiming to bridge the theoretical and empirical dimensions of phonology.

I primarily focus on understudied Chinese dialects, with a particular emphasis on diminutive patterns: the ways languages modify words to make them sound smaller, cuter, or more affectionate. My fascination with these intricate patterns traces back to the local dialects of my hometown and the nostalgic memories of my grandmother's words.
